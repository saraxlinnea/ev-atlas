EV Atlas

Methods

How values enter the table, and where coverage remains incomplete. The longer record is docs/METHODOLOGY.md.

Claims

Each value begins as a claim: field, value, source, access date, and tier. Compare and Charts read a file generated from those claims. A wrong number is corrected in the claim, then regenerated. The CSV is not edited by hand.

When no trustworthy source is found, the cell stays empty. Primary and secondary claims require a concrete URL. Derived values require a formula and inputs. Gaps are left blank rather than estimated.

Source tiers

  • P (primary): OEM page or press kit, EPA, NHTSA, or other government source.
  • S (secondary): Outlet, teardown, or aggregator. Ranked below P even when the figure looks solid.
  • D (derived): Computed from other claims, with formula and inputs recorded.
  • unverified: Plausible but not confirmed. Repetition across sites does not upgrade the tier.

When sources disagree, both claims remain and are marked as conflicts. The table shows the higher tier and keeps the conflict flagged.

EPA and NHTSA

EPA: Per-vehicle records from fueleconomy.gov, checked against the bulk download. The EPA vehicle id appears in each claim's notes. Most rows use the entry-level EPA configuration; notes record which trim or wheel set was selected.

NHTSA recalls: Queried by make, model, and year, not trim. An HTTP 400 with count zero often means the model string did not match, not that no campaigns exist. Zero-recall results are checked against NHTSA's recall flat file.

Known limitations

As of 2026-10-02. Coverage is incomplete; this is not full OEM disclosure for all 20 vehicles.

Tesla and Lucid

  • Lucid Air Pure and Tesla Model Y, Model 3, and Model S have OEM-sourced claims where pages were reachable. Model 3 and Model S figures use Wayback snapshots because live tesla.com blocks scripted fetch.
  • Recorded conflicts include Model 3 MSRP and width, and Model S MSRP and curb weight. Some pack, port, and dimension fields remain secondary-only or missing.
  • Lucid MY2026 Air Pure claims draw on a 2025 press release and Wayback; Lucid's live site shows MY2027. Some specs carry a different model year on the claim.

OEM core

  • Hyundai Ioniq 5 and 6, Kia EV6, Equinox EV, Lyriq, Mach-E, ID.4, EX30, R1T, R2, and Lucid Gravity Touring have OEM claims from fetchable pages.
  • Porsche Taycan, Mercedes CLA Electric, Nissan Leaf, and Chevrolet Bolt later gained OEM buyer fields from dated OEM or newsroom sources. Leaf and Bolt 0–60 remain missing on those pages.
  • BMW i4 eDrive35 is the atlas trim, but BMW USA no longer lists eDrive35 for 2026, so no OEM core was added for that trim.

Model years and availability

  • Most entries are MY2026. Rivian R2 and Chevrolet Bolt are MY2027; Hyundai Ioniq 6 is MY2025 (no MY2026 in EPA or NHTSA).
  • Only chevrolet-bolt-2027 is marked current, from Chevrolet's product page and newsroom. The other 19 remain unverified_availability.

Other caveats

  • NHTSA's products endpoint sometimes lists recalls where no MY2026 campaign exists (Model S, Mach-E, i4); both views are recorded as conflicts.
  • EPA range labels do not state whether range used 5-cycle testing, the 0.7 multiplier, or an approved factor, so range comparisons carry that unknown.
  • Some unverified claims were removed when no re-findable URL existed; those fields stay blank in Compare and on the disclosure chart.
